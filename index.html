I

The House

AURUM was founded in the belief that a perfume is not a product. It is an argument — a position on time, on memory, on what the body can carry.

In 2004, perfumer Léa Voss left a career in haute couture to establish an atelier in the hills above Grasse. She sought fragrances that behaved less like fashion and more like music: things that unfold over time, that demand a second and third encounter before they fully reveal themselves.

AURUM produces a single annual collection. No reformulations. No flankers. Each formula is sealed, numbered, and released in a single edition — after which it retires to the archive.

II

Philosophy

Perfume is the only art that cannot be seen.

We work from a principle we call accord before accord: the agreement between two ingredients, discovered through patient trial, that produces a third thing neither would produce alone. This cannot be computed or accelerated. It requires time — time in the lab, time on the skin, time in a bottle set aside to evolve.

We reject synthetic musks. We do not use ISO E Super, Ambroxide, or any molecule whose ubiquity has made it inaudible. We work with naturals where possible, and when a synthetic is irreplaceable, we use it at concentrations low enough to be felt rather than named.

Our concentration is always extrait — 35–42% aromatic compounds. We see no reason to make a lesser thing.

III

The Garden

Every ingredient in an AURUM formula is traceable to a specific grower, region, and harvest year. The following notes appear in our current collection.

May Rose
Grasse, Provence — 2024

Rosa centifolia, harvested by hand at dawn. Heavy, honeyed, slightly green. The backbone of three current formulas.

Oud Cambodi
Kampong Cham, Cambodia

Agarwood from naturally infected Aquilaria trees. Dense, animalic, almost medicinal. Aged eight years before distillation.

Mysore Sandalwood
Karnataka, India — licensed harvest

Santalum album, now strictly regulated. Creamy, milky, persistent. No substitute has come close.

Enfleurage Tuberose
Grasse, Provence — August

Processed by cold enfleurage — an 18th century technique, slower than steam distillation, but the only method that captures tuberose's narcotic top note intact.

Labdanum
Crete, Greece

A resinous absolute from Cistus ladanifer. Ambery, leathery, with something of the sea. One of perfumery's great base notes.

Bergamot FCF
Reggio Calabria, Italy — 2024

Bergapten-free expression from the Calabrian coast. Precise citrus brightness without photosensitivity risk. Opens most of our brighter compositions.

IV

The Atelier

A formula passes through five stages before it is bottled. Months pass between each.

01

Accord Development

The perfumer begins with a single material — an accord, a pairing — and works outward. Each modification is documented and given time to breathe before evaluation. A formula may see 40–60 iterations in this phase.

02

Wear Testing

All evaluations are done on skin. No blotter strips, no solvent. A formula is applied each morning for three consecutive days before notes are recorded. Worn twice in different climates before advancing.

03

Maceration

The raw concentrate is blended with 96% organic wheat ethanol and sealed. Minimum six weeks maceration; most formulas rest three to four months. The fragrance becomes coherent during this period.

04

Cold Filtration

The macerated fragrance is held at −20°C for 72 hours. Waxes and insoluble molecules precipitate out and are filtered through cellulose. Some translucency is sacrificed for long-term stability.

05

Final Evaluation

Léa Voss evaluates the final batch against the sealed master reference. If the accord has shifted — as naturals always do, year to year — the batch is adjusted or set aside for further maceration.

VI

The Ritual

A perfume at extrait concentration is not applied — it is placed.

The skin must be warm, clean, and lightly moisturised. The pulse points — inside the wrist, behind the ear, at the base of the throat — retain heat that drives the volatilisation of each accord in sequence.

—

Apply one drop. Not two. Wait ten minutes before assessing the opening. Most extraits take longer to open than an EDT; patience is part of the form.

—

Do not rub the wrist against the other. This ruptures the molecular bonds forming the initial accord and produces a result that was never designed.

—

Leave six to eight hours before reapplication. An extrait is not a daily cologne. It is a decision made once in the morning.

—

Store in darkness, below 20°C, away from fluctuating humidity. Keep the bottle full or very nearly so — contact with air accelerates oxidation of the top notes.
